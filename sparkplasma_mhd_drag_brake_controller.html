Electrodynamic & Magnetohydrodynamic Plasma Aerodynamics

Detailed mathematical formulation of active plasma sheath inflation, low-density ionospheric collision kinetics, and the state-observer inversion algorithms preventing virtual sail collapse.

1. Magnetic Deflection & Effective Drag Cross-Section

In low Earth orbit (LEO, 300–600 km), active injection of an electron-ion plasma plume inside an onboard dipole magnetic field $\mathbf{M}$ forms a diamagnetic plasma bubble. The ionized fluid deflects incoming ionospheric ram ions ($\text{O}^+$, $\text{NO}^+$), multiplying the satellite aerodynamic drag force $F_D$ by expanding its effective collision radius $r_{\text{eff}}$ far beyond physical bus geometry:

$$F_D = \frac{1}{2} C_D \rho_{\infty} v_{\text{rel}}^2 \cdot \pi r_{\text{eff}}^2, \quad \text{where} \quad r_{\text{eff}} \approx r_0 \left( \frac{\mu_0 M^2 n_p e^2}{4 \rho_{\infty} v_{\text{rel}}^2 m_i} \right)^{1/6}$$

Here $\rho_{\infty}$ is ambient thermospheric mass density, $v_{\text{rel}} \approx 7.6\text{ km/s}$ is orbital velocity, $M$ is magnetic dipole moment ($A\cdot m^2$), and $n_p$ is injected plasma density. The virtual sail operates without deployable booms or thin mylar films vulnerable to orbital micro-meteoroids.

Key Metric: Effective Area Multiplier $\eta_A = \frac{\pi r_{\text{eff}}^2}{A_{\text{bus}}} \approx 30\text{--}45\times$

2. Ion-Electron Recombination & Bubble Quenching

The fatal failure mode of active plasma brakes is two-body radiative and three-body dielectronic recombination. In regions of high electron density $n_e$ and cold electron temperature $T_e$, charged particles neutralize back into uncharged gas:

$$\frac{dn_e}{dt} = \dot{S}_{\text{arc}} - \alpha_r(T_e) n_e n_i - \nabla \cdot (n_e \mathbf{v}_d) - \nu_{\text{ex}} n_e n_0$$

When recombination rate $\alpha_r(T_e) \propto T_e^{-0.7}$ spikes—common when crossing geomagnetic dip equator where ambient $B$ drops—the net sheath charge $Q$ collapses. Neutralized particles escape unconstrained across magnetic streamlines, dissolving the virtual aerodynamic sail within milliseconds.

Quench Condition: $\tau_{\text{recomb}} = \frac{1}{\alpha_r n_e} < \tau_{\text{transit}} \implies \text{Bubble Collapse}$

3. High-Frequency $(B, Q)$ Boundary State Observer

Flight software executes an onboard Kalman state observer tracking ambient geomagnetic field $\mathbf{B}_{\text{geo}}$ via 3-axis fluxgate magnetometers and total sheath charge $Q(t) = \int I_{\text{sheath}} dt$ via Langmuir return sensors. The dynamic stability boundary is parameterized by:

$$Q_{\text{crit}}(B) = \kappa \cdot \frac{\varepsilon_0 k_B T_e}{e \lambda_D} \exp\left( -\frac{\gamma B}{\sqrt{\rho_{\infty} \mu_0}} \right)$$

When the state vector $(B, Q)$ approaches within a $15\%$ confidence ellipse of $Q_{\text{crit}}$, the controller calculates immediate cathode potential adjustment $\Delta V_{\text{cath}}$ and advances arc discharge frequency $f_{\text{arc}}$ to elevate $T_e$, forcing $\alpha_r \to 0$.

Control Law: $u(t) = -K_P (Q - Q_{\text{crit}}) - K_D \frac{d(Q - Q_{\text{crit}})}{dt} + \mu_{\text{arc}} \Delta B$

4. Spacecraft Hardware & Plasma Arc Architecture

The flight system is modularized into a 1.5U CubeSat subsystem package weighing under $2.2\text{ kg}$, designed to integrate onto commercial smallsats from 12U to 500 kg ESPA-class buses:

$$\eta_{\text{deorbit}} = \frac{\Delta v_{\text{equiv}}}{E_{\text{bus}}} \approx 4.8 \times 10^3 \text{ m/s per kWh} \quad (\gg 10\times \text{ chemical thrusters})$$

Hardware utilizes a solid Teflon or Indium pulsed micro-arc plasma thruster (PPT) with lanthanum hexaboride ($\text{LaB}_6$) hollow cathode emitters, NdFeB passive shielding rings, and radiation-hardened GaN high-frequency H-bridge switching drivers.
